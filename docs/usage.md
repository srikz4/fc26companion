# Using Companion

What each screen tells you and how to work it. One habit powers everything: **save in game** —
every screen refreshes itself the moment you do.

> **If something looks out of date, save in game.** Companion reads your save file; it cannot see
> anything the game has not written yet. Signed a player, played a match, taken a scout report? The
> screen catches up within a few seconds of the next save — advance a day or hit save manually. The
> "synced" clock in the header tells you how long it has been.


The navigation mirrors the game's own menu: **Central · Squad · Transfers · Academy · Office ·
Story · Customise**, each with its own sub-tabs. For the tour rather than the manual, see the
**[feature guide](feature-guide.md)**.

## The controls

- **Desktop — dwell.** Rest the pointer on a control and a bar fills along its edge; when it
  completes, the control fires. On table rows the dwell zone is the dot at the start of the row,
  so the rest of the row is safe to rest a pointer on while you read. Clicking always works too.
- **Phone — tap.** Where there is no hover, dwell is off and everything is a plain tap. Tap
  anything with extra detail to see its tooltip; tap elsewhere to dismiss.
- **Sorting.** Dwell or click any column header; again to flip. Position columns sort in football
  order (GK → defence → attack), never alphabetically.
- **The `i` on a panel heading** carries the explanation of what that panel is and where its
  numbers come from. The panels themselves stay out of your way.
- **Rows open cards.** Open a row anywhere and its card unfolds directly beneath it. The card is
  dressed for the view you opened it from.

## Central

The first screen: today's date and both transfer windows, the season's record, your **league
table**, **your league season**, the competitions you are in, and the transfers the world has
completed.

The table is added up from the save's own record of every match played — played, won, drawn, lost,
goals, points, and the last five results with the most recent on the left. The arrow is places
gained or lost **since the last matchday**, which every club has, including one just promoted; a
`P` or `R` beside a name marks a club that came up or down over the summer. A club three matches
into a run is marked against the number the run is made of — wins burn, defeats freeze, draws sit
level — and five deep is marked harder. **A key under the table names every mark**, whether or not
today's table shows one.

Rows that read *not yet named* are explained in [what Companion does not
show](#what-companion-does-not-show).

**Your league season** lists your fixtures in date order — venue, opponent, scoreline, and what is
next. **Around Europe** is the matchday round-up as the save recorded it.

Down the side: **Decisions** (every rule-driven action), the **Treatment room**, **Movers**,
**Pulse**, the opponent edge — which now picks your next opponent by itself when the fixture list
names them — and, with RPG mode on, the campaign.

The treatment room shows who is out and how many days are left of it. It suggests cover **only for
someone who was in the XI you saved**: a player you have dropped to the reserves leaves no gap in
the team, so naming a replacement for him would be inventing a problem.

## Squad

- **Squad Hub** — the roster in four views that share one table: **Basic** (position, rating,
  ceiling, age, season change, the action to take), **Stats** (rated matches, average rating,
  minutes, form), **Attributes** (group means per player), **Financial** (wage, contract, wage
  verdict, EA value, what this world pays). Open a row and the card matches the view: the full
  attribute sheet under Attributes, the match log under Stats, the money page under Financial.

  Beside each name are the marks: injured, away with his country, a standout ceiling, and how he is
  playing. **A key under the roster names every one**, roles included, so a shield on a
  centre-back is not read as the same claim as a flame on a striker.

  Season **appearances and goals are not shown** — see [what Companion does not
  show](#what-companion-does-not-show). "Rated" is the number of matches behind the average, which
  is the size of the window the save keeps, not a season total.
- **Team Management** — a **depth chart** first (every position, first choice down, one-injury-from-
  a-hole flagged), then your saved XI against the XI the fit engine would pick on a pitch drawn
  from your own formation's coordinates, the selection differences, set-piece takers, shape
  comparison, the opponent scout, and **Bench** and **Reserves** as real tables.
- **Development** — everyone with growth left as runway cards: how far he has come against his
  ceiling, whether the minutes are arriving, and the attributes where growth buys the most fit.
- **Synergy** — who supplies whom, how strongly, and what it means, as connection cards; then the
  three biggest levers, and every scored pair in the squad.
- **Wages** — the bill and what each role band pays, then a card per player: current wage, the
  offer, the term, the contract runway, and whether he is paid with or against his band. Open one
  for both packages (flat, and a lower base with appearance money) and the release-clause call.
  **Every player with a recorded wage gets a proposal** — when to renew is your call, not the app's.
- **Coach** — the best-rated club managers in your leagues, from the game's own star ratings.

## Transfers

- **Targets** — players your career can actually sign, grouped into Superstars / In the making /
  Wonderkids / Underdogs, with fit and synergy computed against **your** squad and the case for
  each signing as reason badges. Open a row for the player's full sheet and the **negotiation
  guide**: what to open at, what it will probably take, the point where you are paying over the
  odds, and the wage he expects with the room either side.

  Those come from the fees clubs in your world have actually paid, not from the valuation curve —
  what a player is *worth* and what a club will *accept* are different questions, and the second is
  usually lower. **Trust the floor more than the middle**: measured against two real negotiations,
  the price the selling club would have taken came in at or just above "open at". Your own signings
  are excluded from the model, since training it on your offers would only make it agree with you.

  A row labelled with a `~` means the player is beyond anything this world has traded, so the
  number is the curve continued past its evidence. The tooltip names the rating range it does stand
  on.
- **Shortlist** — the shortlist exactly as the game saved it, read from the save itself, and
  **dated**. The game rewrites that section only now and then — measured at nearly four months
  stale on a live career — so a player you have just dropped in game can still be listed. The date
  on the panel is the save's, not today's.
- **Watchlist** — Companion's own list. Starring a player freezes his numbers, and the row then
  shows the drift plus a verdict that moves with every save (*still worth it / move now / cooling /
  ceiling reached*).
- **Sell Values** — an EA-style valuation for every senior player alongside what this world's own
  deals have paid. Both bands are built from fees that were *paid*, which is the buying side of the
  table, so **a sale you drive usually clears higher**: on the one sale measured against real
  figures, the top of the band was 40M, the game's own delegate quoted 60M, and it went for 47.5M.
  Treat the ceiling as where to open, not where to stop.
- **Loans** — who is out and the only performance signal the save records (rating and ceiling
  movement since July); who should go out, with the clubs where their level makes them a starter;
  and a **scorer for a real approach** — pick the player and the club that called, and the offer is
  scored out of ten from line strength, stretch, league quality and the development profile.

## Academy

- **My Academy** — the prospects you have signed, in the same four views as the Squad Hub, so a
  16-year-old has a full attribute sheet like anyone else.
- **Scout Reports** — your scouts, their missions and return dates, and every prospect a scout has
  delivered that you have not signed yet, each with a **sign / watch / pass** verdict weighed against
  the academy you already have and the positions that are thin. Open a row for the full attribute
  sheet. A delivered report sits in your academy squad without a contract, and signing him in game is
  what writes one — that is exactly how Companion tells reports from your own prospects.

## Office

- **Board** — where you stand, and this season's competitions.
- **Manager** — the career as a timeline (one column per season, height by points, trophies
  attached), the season record, and your record book.
- **Manager Market** — every real manager in the world by the game's own star rating, split into
  the men's game, the women's game, and the unattached.
- **Finances** — budgets (labelled honestly where the save does not persist them), club worth, the
  wage bill, and the club's prestige and youth-development ratings as bars.
- **Club Stats** — position strength, age profile, scorers, ratings, minutes, season growth, and
  **Ceiling watch** (dynamic potential, observed from your own snapshots — falls first).
- **Challenges** — the game's live scenarios are not written to the save; the tab says so.

## Story

- **Chronicle** — your career as chapters, written only from the record: the finish, the trophies,
  the record scorelines, the transfer business, promotions out of the academy, and players crossing
  80, 85 and 90. Events are recorded in a **story ledger** the first time Companion sees them, and
  never rewritten — so history keeps its own dates instead of being recomputed from the present.
  Seasons that were already in the save when you started carry no date, because none is known.
- **Brag card** — the shareable 1080-wide career report. **Save as image** downloads a PNG; **Copy
  caption** puts a paste-ready text on the clipboard.
- **Campaign** — with RPG mode on: the career arc as a stepper (done / you are here / ahead), this
  season's phase missions, and the marks within reach right now.

## Customise

Grouped switches for Display, Central panels, Guidance and Preferences (landing tab, compact
density, full money figures, player faces), plus a box each for the two modes.

**Save file** is the one setting here that is not a browser preference. Companion follows the
newest Manager Career save the game writes, which is what you want while playing — but a save is
just a file, and one restored from a backup or copied off another machine reads just as well. The
panel lists what it can find and takes a full path for anything else.

Choosing a file **moves the watcher onto that file's folder**, so a save written there — by the
game or by anything else — still lands on screen by itself. The choice is remembered across
restarts, and a file that later moves or is deleted is quietly forgotten rather than breaking
startup.

**Favour youth in the XI** (under Guidance) breaks near-ties toward the younger player when
Companion picks an eleven. It is worth a fraction of a rating point and never enough to pick a weak
player: on a live squad it swapped a 30-year-old 85 for a 22-year-old 84 and cost the side a tenth
of a point. Off — the default — the XI is simply the strongest side today.

**RPG mode** turns the career into a campaign: pick one of nine — or blend several under Custom,
where their ladders interleave so the arc advances on every front at once. Missions appear in the
campaign's violet at the top of whichever view the work belongs to — title pace on Team Management,
minutes on Development, contracts on Wages — and each campaign names the phases of a season in its
own voice. **AI mode** is switched off and unavailable: nothing is
wired behind it yet, and the app does not pretend otherwise.

## The alert rail

"Needs attention" collects every action the rules fired — renewals expiring, prospects starved of
minutes, ceilings slipping, academy deals running down — each with the numbers that produced it.
Hide keeps it out of the way during play.

## Honesty rules, so you can trust the screen

- `~` marks a derived value (an estimated date, a derived name, an EA-style valuation, a fee beyond
  the evidence) — shown, never used to drive advice.
- A dash means the save does not hold that fact. Companion does not fill gaps with guesses.
- Fits, fees and wages come from models fitted on your world. Asked about a player outside anything
  that world has traded, a model still answers and **labels the answer** as the curve continued
  past its evidence, naming the range it does stand on.
- History starts when the watcher does. Anything the save already contained is read from its own
  record and carries no invented date.

## What Companion does not show

Some things the game displays are not in the save in any form that can be read. They are left out
rather than approximated, and this is the list.

**Season appearances and goals.** The save's own fields disagree with the game's screen —
`leagueappearances` reads zero for every player while the game shows twenty-odd, and `leaguegoals`
gave a striker 11 where the game said 14 in the league and 25 overall. A controlled experiment
settled it: one match, one player, one goal, a save either side. No database column moved for him
beyond a new match-rating row; no bytes near his id changed like a counter. What the save keeps is
a **rolling fifteen-match window** per player — it dropped its oldest date and added the new one —
and the game rebuilds the totals in memory. Which is why the tools that do track them read the
running game rather than the file. Average rating and minutes are shown, because those come from
that window and do agree with the game.

**Suspensions.** The blob holds a ledger of bans with the player, the club and the number of
matches, but it reads as a season history rather than a live list, and there has been no chance to
check it against a known suspension. Until there is, the treatment room shows injuries only.

**Club names on some league rows.** The save files fixtures by participant slot, not by club, and
keeps no map between them. A slot is named when a save proves whose it is — from a matchday
round-up, or from who a club you can already name just played. Run `npm run backfill:fixtures` to
read those proofs out of every save already archived; one season's worth usually names a whole
division in a single pass.

**Most of the world news feed.** One entry kind is understood — a transfer, which names both clubs.
The rest carry a player, a club and a date but nothing saying what happened, so they are counted
rather than captioned with a number that would mean nothing.

**Cup and continental tables.** The league table is built for whichever division you manage in, so
it works the same in La Liga as in the Premier League. Other competitions in the fixture ledger
need their slots named too, and the round-up only covers domestic leagues.

# Companion — the feature guide

**A read-only second screen for EA SPORTS FC 26 Manager Career.**
It watches your save, parses it the moment the game writes it, and shows you the career the game
keeps to itself — on your PC, or on your phone over your own Wi-Fi.

Everything on screen comes out of **your** save file. Nothing is invented. A fact the save does not
hold renders as unknown, and anything derived wears a `~`.

---

## The first screen

![Central](images/central.svg)

Central is the day in front of you: the date, both transfer windows with the open one lit, and the
season's record. Down the side sit the decisions the rules have fired, who is injured, who is
moving, and — if you want it — the campaign.

**The league table is real.** FC 26 keeps no standings anywhere in the save; it keeps every fixture
and every scoreline and adds them up on the way to the screen, and so does Companion. Played, won,
drawn, lost, goals, points — arithmetic over results the save recorded, checked row for row against
the game's own table. Below it, your whole season: every fixture with its date, venue, kick-off and
scoreline, and what is next.

A club three matches into a run is marked against the number the run is made of — **wins burn,
defeats freeze, draws sit level** — and five deep is marked harder. The arrow is places gained or
lost since the last matchday, which every club has, promoted ones included. A key under the table
names every mark whether or not today's table shows one, because the moment you first meet a symbol
is exactly when its explanation needs to be on screen.

Cover for an injured player is only suggested when he was in the XI you saved. A player you have
dropped to the reserves leaves no gap in the team, and naming a replacement for him would be
inventing a problem to solve.

The dot beside the clock is the one thing worth learning: **green** means what you are reading is
current, **amber** that it is ten minutes old, **red** that it is half an hour old and the game has
moved on without you. Companion can only ever be as fresh as your last save.

## Every player, four ways

![Player card](images/player-card.svg)

The Squad Hub is one table with four column sets — **Basic**, **Stats**, **Attributes**,
**Financial** — and opening a row unfolds a card *dressed for the view you opened it from*: the full
attribute sheet here, the match-by-match log under Stats, the money page under Financial.

Every attribute, exactly as the game shows them, keepers included. Season change rides beside each
number, and the chips at the bottom mark what he is genuinely elite at for his position — measured
against the 21,000 players in **your** save's world, not a global average.

The academy gets the same four views, so a sixteen-year-old is as readable as your captain.

**How he is playing** rides beside the name, in the same language as the league table: a hat-trick
burns hardest, then a brace, then a scoring run; below that it is match ratings, and the badge
follows the role, because a centre-back who has not been beaten in three is not making the same
claim as a striker on a run. The ratings are the ones the game itself gave, over the window the
save keeps, and unused substitutes are not counted — a rating with no minutes behind it is not a
performance.

Season appearances and goals are **not** shown. The save's own fields for them disagree with the
game's screen, and a controlled experiment — one match, one goal, a save either side — confirmed
nothing in the file moves when a player scores. See *Honesty* below.

**Position fit.** Every player is rated in every slot by a model fitted on your own world and
anchored so that a player's rating in his own position matches the game exactly. When it says a
midfielder is an 84 at right-back, that number means something.

## What he is worth

![Sell values](images/valuation.svg)

The save holds no player value — so Companion rebuilds EA's own valuation curves (overall × age ×
ceiling × position) for a **fair value with a floor and a ceiling**. Alongside it sits what your
world's own completed transfers have actually paid for that profile.

They answer different questions, and the difference is the point. A valuation says what a player is
**worth**; a negotiation is about what a club will **accept**, which is usually less. So a target's
card carries a guide built from real fees, not from the curve: **open at, likely to take, over the
odds above** — and the wage he expects with the room either side. Measured against two real
negotiations, the price the selling club would have taken came in at or just above *open at*, so
the floor is the number to trust.

Your own signings are kept out of that model. It exists to tell you whether an offer is sane, and
training it on your own offers would only make it agree with you — on the save this was built
against, including them priced a comparable player at 115M when the club would have taken 74M.

Where a player is beyond anything your world has traded, the number is still shown and labelled
`~`, with the rating range the model does stand on. Selling runs the other way: those bands come
from fees that were *paid*, so a sale you drive tends to clear above them, and the panel says so.

The same thinking runs through **Wages**: every player with a recorded wage gets a proposal — two
package shapes (flat, or a lower base with appearance money), a release-clause verdict, and the
longest term the game will accept at his age. When to renew is your call; Companion never hides the
option.

## The career as a campaign

![Campaign](images/campaign.svg)

Switch on **RPG mode** and the career becomes a story with rules. Nine campaigns — Road to Glory,
The Treble, Invincibles, Century Club, The Wall, Youth Revolution, Academy Project, Moneyball — or a
**custom blend** of any of them, whose ladders interleave so the arc advances on every front at once
instead of finishing one story before starting the next.

Three tiers you can tell apart at a glance:

- **The arc** — career milestones as a stepper: done, *you are here*, still ahead.
- **This season** — missions re-cut with the phase of the season, named in the campaign's own voice.
  August is "Loading the guns" under Century Club and "Buy low" under Moneyball.
- **Right now** — the marks within reach this week.

And it is a **lens, not a tab**: with RPG on, each mission appears in violet at the top of the view
where the work actually happens — title pace on Team Management, minutes on Development, contracts
on Wages. Every number is computed from your save. Nothing is rolled.

## The story it remembers

![Chronicle](images/chronicle.svg)

Companion keeps a **story ledger**: trophies, finishes, record scorelines, transfer business,
promotions out of the academy, and players crossing 80, 85 and 90 — each written the first time it
is seen and never rewritten. The Chronicle reads that back as chapters.

That "written once" matters. History keeps its own dates instead of being recomputed from the
present, so a record set two seasons ago stays filed where it happened. Seasons that were already in
your save when you started carry no date, because none is knowable — Companion would rather say
nothing than date last year with today.

## How it behaves

**Dwell, not clicking.** On a desktop, rest the pointer on a control and a bar fills; when it
completes, the control fires. On table rows the dwell zone is the dot at the row's start, so the
rest of the row is safe to rest a pointer on while you read. Clicking always works too. On a phone
there is no hover, so everything is a plain tap.

**One look.** The app wears the game's own language — near-black, volt green — with a violet reserved
entirely for the story layer and a cyan for modes that are not yet available.

**It explains itself once.** Panels state their name and show their numbers; the `i` on a heading
carries the explanation of where those numbers came from, for the moments you want it.

## Honesty, which is the point

- `~` marks a derived value. It is shown, and it never drives a recommendation.
- A dash means the save does not hold that fact. Gaps are not filled with guesses.
- **A model says where its evidence stops.** Asked about a player beyond anything your world has
  traded, it still answers — a labelled guess beats a blank — and names the range it does stand on,
  because the curve is exponential and a rating or two past the data moves the answer by tens of
  millions.
- Features with nothing behind them say so. AI mode is switched **off and marked unavailable**
  rather than shipping a plausible placeholder.
- **Numbers that disagree with the game are removed, not adjusted.** Season appearances and goals
  used to be shown from the save's own fields; those fields read zero appearances for every player
  while the game showed twenty-odd, and gave a striker 11 league goals against the game's 14. They
  are gone. Average rating and minutes stayed, because they come from the save's match record and
  do agree.
- When the game keeps something Companion cannot read, it says exactly that. The league table is
  the clearest case: the standings are added up from the save's own record of every match played,
  but the save files those matches by slot rather than by club. A slot is named only when a save
  proves it — from the matchday round-up, or from who a club you can already name just played. Rows
  nothing has named yet read **not yet named**, and fill in as the season goes on.
  `npm run backfill:fixtures` reads the names out of every save already archived, which usually
  completes a division in one go.

### What was looked for and not found

Three things the game shows that are genuinely not in the file, each checked rather than assumed:

- **Season statistics.** Every table in the save was enumerated — sixty-six across two database
  blocks, of which exactly one is absent from the community schema, and it is empty. Then a
  controlled experiment: one match, one player, one goal, a save either side. No column moved for
  him beyond a new match-rating row; no bytes near his id changed like a counter. What the save
  keeps is a rolling **fifteen-match window** per player, which dropped its oldest entry and gained
  the new one. The game rebuilds the totals in memory, which is why the tools that do track them
  read the running game rather than the file.
- **Suspensions.** There is a ledger of bans in the blob carrying the player, the club and a number
  of matches, but it reads as a season history rather than a live list, and no known suspension has
  been available to test it against.
- **Most of the world news feed.** One entry kind is understood — a transfer, which names both
  clubs. The rest carry a player, a club and a date but nothing saying what happened. They are
  counted rather than captioned.

## Getting it running

Ten minutes, once. See **[install.md](install.md)** — you need Node.js, `npm install`, and a
double-click of `Companion.vbs`. Phone access is one flag and prints its own address.

**Read-only, local-only.** Companion never writes to a save, never touches game memory, never
injects. No accounts, no cloud, no telemetry; the server binds to your machine, and phone access on
your own network is an explicit opt-in.

Then see **[usage.md](usage.md)** for what each screen means once you are in.
